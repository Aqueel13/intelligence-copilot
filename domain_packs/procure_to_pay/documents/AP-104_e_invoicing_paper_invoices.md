# E-invoicing and Paper Invoice Handling

Varaha Auto Components Pvt. Ltd. (fictional)

## 1. Purpose

Sets how suppliers must submit invoices, and how paper invoices are handled until every supplier submits electronically.

## 2. Legal background

2.1 Under GST rules, a supplier whose aggregate annual turnover exceeds **₹5 crore** must issue an **e-invoice** with an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) for business-to-business supplies.

2.2 Smaller suppliers may still send PDF or paper invoices.

## 3. Rule for suppliers

3.1 Suppliers required to e-invoice must submit invoices through the **supplier portal**, which reads the IRN and needs no manual header capture.

3.2 Paper invoices from these suppliers are to be **returned to the supplier** with a request to resubmit through the portal.

3.3 All other suppliers are encouraged to use the portal or e-mail (PDF).

## 4. Paper invoice handling

4.1 Paper invoices are received at each plant's **mailroom**, logged, and sent by the **weekly courier (Thursday)** to central scanning in Pune. Scanning is completed within 2 working days of arrival in Pune. Only then can the invoice be captured (AP-102).

4.2 A paper invoice therefore usually waits **three to seven working days** before capture, which uses up much of the ten-day payment target (AP-001).

4.3 **Alternative:** with the AP Manager's approval, a plant may scan paper invoices **at its own mailroom on the day they arrive**, removing the courier and central scanning wait.

## 5. Enforcement and status

5.1 The AP Manager owns this policy and decides on returning paper invoices and on plant scanning. The Chief Financial Officer may also decide.

5.2 §3.2 has **not yet been enforced**: the supplier onboarding drive was postponed. Paper invoices are about one in ten overall, and far more at Sanand, whose local job-work suppliers mostly invoice on paper (REF-401).

5.3 No plant scans paper invoices locally at present.
